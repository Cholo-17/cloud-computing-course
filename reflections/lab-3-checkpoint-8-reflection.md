# Mission 3 Reflection

Completing Mission 3 helped me understand that choosing a cloud provider is not simply about choosing the most popular platform. AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform all provide many similar cloud services, but each platform has different strengths that can make it more appropriate for a particular organization.

The cloud platform that impressed me the most was AWS. I found AWS interesting because it provides a very large selection of services for computing, storage, databases, networking, security, and other cloud requirements. Its wide range of services makes it useful for both small startups and large organizations.

AWS, Azure, and GCP have many similarities. All three provide virtual machines, object storage, databases, networking, identity management, containers, and other cloud services. They also provide global infrastructure that allows organizations to deploy applications in different geographic locations. Although the names of their services are different, many services perform similar functions.

Business requirements have an important influence on cloud platform selection. For example, a company that already depends heavily on Microsoft products may benefit from Azure because of its integration with Microsoft's technologies. An AI research company may prefer Google Cloud because of its AI and machine learning capabilities. A startup may select AWS because of its wide selection of scalable services.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that technical decisions should be connected to the customer's actual needs. Instead of selecting a platform based only on popularity, I learned to consider factors such as existing technology, scalability, services, and business requirements.

This mission also improved my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio. I learned how to organize cloud research into separate Markdown files and document technical information in a structured way. The portfolio now demonstrates not only my knowledge of cloud platforms but also my ability to research, compare, and make technical recommendations.
